Why the same seed makes different worlds

A source-level audit of world-generation nondeterminism in GT:NH 1.7.10 — six confirmed mechanisms ranked by routing impact, cross-referenced against the #run-determinism Discord archive, each with a concrete fix.

scope: overworld structures, TF, GC, biomes method: direct source reads + decompiled Witchery/TC4 date: 2026-07-22

0Summary

Village positions are seed-stable. What breaks routing is everything layered on top: village layouts scramble on every game launch because of a Forge registry bug; Witchery rolls its surface structures off a clock-seeded RNG; Thaumcraft's node and structure gen shifts with chunk exploration order; GregTech veins can still swap identity near caves; Roguelike Dungeons pick their spot by probing terrain that may or may not exist yet. Terrain and biome layout themselves are clean — which matches what runners observe.

1How determinism actually breaks here

Forge 1.7.10 re-seeds the shared populate RNG before each registered world generator, so one mod cannot shift another mod's stream directly. Every confirmed bug works through one of four mechanisms:

A · clock-seeded RNGRandomness that never saw the seed. new Random(), Math.random(), or the global world.rand (seeded from the clock at world load) used during generation. Different every run, full stop.
B · conditional draw skewA branch that consumes draws only sometimes. When the branch condition depends on anything non-seed-pure, every consumer after it reads a shifted stream. This is the mental model the community already had — and it shows up in Thaumcraft in exactly that form.
C · chunk-order dependenceReading world state that may not exist yet. Probing blocks in neighbor chunks, or caching a decision from whichever chunk generates first. Walk toward a structure from the east instead of the north and the probe sees different blocks.
D · unstable iteration orderHash-ordered registries. HashMap<Class, …> iterates in identity-hash order, which changes every JVM launch — stable within a session, scrambled across restarts.

Chunk population order is driven by player movement, so mechanism C means "which direction you approach from" is itself an input to worldgen. This also explains the community's split observations: things that are "consistent within a session but not across restarts" are mechanism D; things that differ "even within the same session" are A or C.

2Confirmed findings

F1Village layouts scramble every launch — a Forge bug, not a mod bug

mechanism Dstable in-session, new every launchconfidence: verified in source

Forge's VillagerRegistry stores village building handlers in HashMap<Class<?>, IVillageCreationHandler> (VillagerRegistry.java:49). When a village start piece assembles its building list, addExtraVillageComponents (:211) iterates .values() — identity-hash order, reshuffled every JVM launch. Two effects compound:

  • Handlers consume draws from the village layout RNG as they're visited (TiC's tool station rolls random.nextInt(4), Witchery's apothecary rolls conditionally), so the draw order — and everything after it — changes per launch.
  • Vanilla's piece picker walks the weighted list in order, subtracting weights, so even draw-free handlers change which building a given roll selects.

The pack registers handlers from Witchery (~6), VillageNames (~6), TinkersConstruct (2, including the smeltery), Railcraft, ForestryMC, and WitchingGadgets — a large map, thoroughly scrambled. Village positions come from seeded math in MapGenVillage and are unaffected.

Contamination

In RWG's populate (ChunkGeneratorRealistic.java:474-524) villages place their blocks from the same shared RNG that then feeds vanilla dungeons, lakes, ores, and tree decoration. A different layout consumes a different number of draws — so in any chunk a village touches, all of those shift too.

Discord cross-reference

  • Raven, 2022: removing Witchery made villages "generate in the same spot every time" — Witchery was the biggest handler contributor in that minimal test, not the root cause.
  • exfret: "village layouts aren't completely random… they take one of a selection of forms" — the forms are permutations of handler iteration order.
  • lhaxq, 2025 (pack 2.7): "cannot get the village that used to have a smeltery to spawn with one again" — the smeltery is literally one of the scrambled handlers. Any change to the pack's mod set re-deals the deck, which is why 2.7 "broke" villages without touching village code.

Fix

Small and pack-wide: a Hodgepodge mixin that makes addExtraVillageComponents iterate handlers in a deterministic order (sort by class name). One-file change, fixes every mod's village pieces at once. A stricter variant also hands each handler a forked RNG seeded from the layout seed + handler name, so a future mod can't skew its neighbors.

F2Witchery rolls structures off the clock and builds walls on a timer

mechanism A + Cdifferent every runconfidence: verified (decompiled 0.24.1)

Witchery is closed-source; findings are from decompiling the shipped jar.

  • Surface structures. WitcheryWorldGenerator.generate discards the seeded per-chunk RNG Forge hands it and passes world.rand — the global, clock-seeded World random — into all overworld generation, then calls Collections.shuffle(generators, random) with it. Covens, wicker men, shacks, and goblin huts land at seed-gated grid cells (that part is seeded), but whether and what actually spawns there is rolled off the clock. Nondeterministic every run.
  • Village walls. Village gen plants an invisible spawner block whose tile entity waits 40+ ticks, then builds the walls from whatever the world looks like at that moment, only replacing blocks that are currently replaceable (WorldHandlerVillageDistrict$Wall). Wall shape therefore depends on chunk-load and tick timing — this is why runners see walls pop in inside render distance, and why exfret found them inconsistent even within a session.

Fix

Witchery is ARR, so fixes go in a mixin host (WitcheryExtras already ships mixins into it). Redirect generateOverworld's RNG to a seed-derived one (the AE2 grid-seed pattern maps 1:1: seed the cell decision from worldSeed + cellX/cellZ), and build walls at generation time from the village's own seeded RNG instead of a delayed tile entity.

F3Thaumcraft: terrain-gated draws, clock loot, and a thread in worldgen

mechanism A + B + Cdiffers even within a sessionconfidence: verified (TC 4.2.3.5 sources)

TC's generator does use the seeded per-chunk RNG — but almost every feature gates further draws on live terrain reads, at un-offset coordinates that neighbor-chunk decoration may already have altered:

  • generateTotem (ThaumcraftWorldGenerator.java:118-170): rolls position, then reads getHeightValue/getBlock; only if the column looks right does it consume more draws and place a node. One flipped gate shifts every later TC draw in the chunk — textbook conditional draw skew.
  • generateWildNodes (:172-204): same shape — terrain reads decide whether extra nextInt calls happen before createRandomNodeAt consumes a long, variable draw chain.
  • Barrow loot rolls straight off world.rand (WorldGenMound.java:2508+) — urn/crate contents are clock-random.
  • The scattered-feature bonus node is deduped through an in-memory map keyed by position hash (:421-426) — which chunk claims it depends on population order, and the map resets every session.
  • Eldritch ring generation spawns a background thread (new Thread(new MazeThread(…)).start(), :456-457) whose writes race the main generation thread.

Net effect: node types/positions, totems, barrows, and rings vary run-to-run and route-to-route — matching rakdarian's "all thaumcraft structures are non-deterministic even in the same session." Taint biomes are decided in the biome layer, which is seed-pure — matching Pao's observation that they are stable.

Fix

Largest job on the list, best done incrementally in Salis-Arcana (which already mixins into TC worldgen classes): give each feature its own RNG forked from worldSeed + chunk coords + feature id so terrain gates can't skew siblings; replace world.rand loot rolls with the feature RNG; make the bonus-node dedup a pure function of position; run maze gen synchronously with a seeded RNG.

F4GT ore veins can still reroll — the retry probe reads live terrain

mechanism Crare; near caves, ravines, mountainsidesconfidence: verified in current source

Modern GT5U worldgen is otherwise exemplary — every entry point reseeds from worldSeed + coordinates and vein geometry is cached so all chunks of a vein agree. But the placement-attempt loop survives from the old code: testWorldgenChunkified probes a 9-block column of live world blocks at the triggering chunk's center (WorldgenGTOreLayer.java:239-251); fewer than 5 stone hits returns NO_OVERLAP_AIR_BLOCK, and the driver retries with the next vein type (GTWorldgenerator.java:465-478). The first chunk of the vein region to generate caches the winner for the whole vein.

So when some chunks of a vein region have cavey/exposed probe columns and others don't, the vein's identity (and its resolved height) depends on which chunk you cause to generate first — i.e., your approach direction. This is exactly the copper↔iron reroll Leosha diagnosed and exfret hit in 2023; it's rare because most probe columns are solid stone.

Fix

Make the probe seed-pure: drop the world read and accept placement (veins in air pockets are harmless), or probe the chunk's own freshly generated (pre-population) block array instead of the live world. Small, contained change.

F5Roguelike Dungeons choose their spot by probing terrain that may not exist yet

mechanism Cposition varies within its regionconfidence: verified in source

The spawn region is seed-gated (same grid trick Witchery uses, Dungeon.java:120-143) — but the exact position is found by up to 50 attempts of getNearbyCoord, each candidate 40–100 blocks away, validated by validLocation (:165-211) which reads live blocks over 9×9 areas deep inside neighboring chunks. Whether a candidate passes depends on whether those chunks are terrain-only or already decorated — so the dungeon's final position (and therefore its position-seeded layout and loot, via the axis-collapsing seed * x * z at :227-231) is approach-order-dependent.

Fix

Validate against seed-pure data (biome + noise-derived height) instead of live blocks, or restrict probing to the triggering chunk. Also worth replacing seed*x*z (zero on axes, x↔z symmetric) with a proper hash while in there.

F6RWG trees: clock-sized big trees and gate-skewed pines

mechanism A + B + Ccosmetic, but contaminatesconfidence: verified in source

Two distinct bugs in the pack's own overworld generator:

  • DecoBigTree's no-arg constructor sizes the tree with Math.random() (DecoBigTree.java:37), used in WoodHills/WoodMountains decoration — clock-random geometry.
  • DecoLargePine (DecoLargePine.java:33-36) and family return before any draw unless the ground block is grass/dirt — a terrain gate on the shared decoration RNG. Tree trunks and branches also write across chunk borders unclamped, cascading state into neighbors and flipping their gates (including TC's totem checks and GT's vein probes).

This is the user-observed DecoLargePine/cherry-blossom variance. Tree shapes themselves don't matter for routing, but the skew propagates to every later consumer of that chunk's decoration RNG.

Fix

Replace the Math.random() ctor with the passed RNG; make each tree consume a fixed draw shape (roll first, gate after — one nextLong() to fork a private RNG per tree is the cheap idiom, already used correctly by TC's greatwood and RWG's own DecoBigTree.generate).

F7Smaller residuals

mixed mechanismslocalized impact
  • NewHorizonsCoreMod: ZincGravelWorldgen.java:47-49 misses the +8 decoration offset (cascading-gen class — the same bug Hodgepodge fixes for BOP kelp); OilGeneratorFix.buildOilStructure reads/writes neighbor-chunk state during populate. Decision RNG for both is seed-pure; only boundary blocks vary.
  • Vanilla dungeons (spawner rooms, via RWG populate): placement checks read blocks near chunk edges — mild order dependence, was in vanilla too.
  • Twilight Forest: the GTNH fork's decorator is properly rewritten; the residual is getHeightValue reads that can see neighbor-chunk trees, skewing decoration draws near borders. TF landmark positions are seeded math — clean.
  • Forestry: WorldGenPadauk.java:47-51 uses three fresh new Random() for branch geometry — clock-random tree shape, no stream contamination.
  • amunra (space dims): Pyramid.java:452-473 picks room entrances with Math.random() — pyramid interiors differ per run.
  • BloodMagic: TEDemonPortal picks buildings via new Random() + HashMap key iteration — but it's ritual-triggered at runtime, not worldgen; irrelevant to seed routing.

3Audited and cleared

  • ✓ AE2 meteors — eigenraven's 2022 fix (grid-cell seeding + persisted per-meteor seed, MeteoriteWorldGen.java:44-88) is correct and complete; it's the template the other fixes should copy.
  • ✓ Terrain & biome layout — RWG is pure seeded noise; Climate Control hooks vanilla's GenLayer init, which the rwg world type never calls, so it can't perturb the overworld.
  • ✓ GT veins otherwise — coordinate-derived seeds everywhere, ordered ArrayList registries, per-vein caching. F4 is the one surviving hole.
  • ✓ Galacticraft Moon/Mars dungeons — generated per-chunk from block arrays with a seed-pure position and layout seed. (Weak seeding — seed*x*z collapses on axes — but identically every run.)
  • ✓ Village positions, TF landmarks, strongholds, mineshafts — vanilla seeded MapGen math.
  • ✓ TC taint biomes — biome-layer decision, seed-pure (matches field reports).
  • ✓ Chest loot — deterministic given the structure layout; it only looks random because F1 changes which building the chest is in.

4Verifying in-game

For any candidate fix, the test that matters distinguishes the two nondeterminism classes:

  • Launch test (mechanism A/D): generate the seed, note the target (village layout, blacksmith), quit the game process, relaunch, regenerate. F1-class bugs flip here; a same-session regen will falsely look stable.
  • Approach test (mechanism B/C): two fresh worlds, same seed, same session — but walk to the target from opposite directions (spiral out east-first vs north-first). GT vein rerolls, Roguelike positions, and TC structures flip here.
  • Compare with VisualProspecting exports or a region-file diff rather than eyeballing; AmidstGTNH helps for macro placement.
Rule of thumb from this audit: stable in-session but different after relaunch → hash-order bug (F1). Different even in-session → clock RNG or chunk-order bug (F2–F6).

6What players will notice

One-time cost: the fixes change how randomness is derived, so the first generation on fixed jars produces a different world for a given seed than stock did — old seed notes reset once, then the seed is stable forever, for everyone. Existing saves are safe: only newly generated chunks are affected.

What becomes reliable: village layouts (blacksmith/smeltery presence becomes a property of the seed), village chest loot, Witchery covens/wicker men/shacks/goblin huts, RWG tree geometry, GT vein identity near caves, and — with the F3 draft — Thaumcraft nodes, totems, barrows, ring placement, and barrow container types.

What still varies: Witchery village walls (tick-time build), Roguelike dungeon position within its seed-fixed region, block-level border effects from vanilla-class bugs, and the unidentified residual above. Macro placement is stable; individual blocks near chunk seams may not be.

In practice: practice worlds match run worlds; seeds become shareable, verifiable claims; set-seed categories become viable with automated verification (two runners' probe hashes should match chunk-for-chunk minus documented residuals). Determinism is per mod-set — pack version changes still legitimately re-roll seeds.

7Fix priority

  1. VillagerRegistry ordering mixin (F1) — one small Hodgepodge mixin; deterministic village layouts across the whole pack. Highest payoff per line of code, and it's upstream-Forge-clean.
  2. Witchery RNG redirect (F2) — mixin in WitcheryExtras; seeded grid decisions for surface structures, walls built at gen time.
  3. GT vein probe (F4) — small GT5U PR; make the air-block probe seed-pure.
  4. RWG trees (F6) — small RWG PR; fixes the visible tree variance and stops decoration-stream contamination.
  5. Roguelike validLocation (F5) — medium; seed-pure placement.
  6. Thaumcraft rework (F3) — largest; stage it through Salis-Arcana, loot rolls first (cheap), node placement second.
